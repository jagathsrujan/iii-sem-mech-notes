Strength of Materials · Unit 2

Shear Force and Bending Moment

A beam carries loads across a gap and passes them to its supports. This unit shows how to find the shear force and the bending moment at every section of a beam and how to draw them as diagrams (SFD and BMD). The maximum bending moment you find here is the input for bending stress (next unit) and for deflection (Unit 4).

Concepts

Exam pattern. In the old UG-2022 papers this topic is one full unit with two questions (choose one), 20 marks each. Part (a) is usually a standard case "with usual notations" (simply supported beam with UDL or UVL, cantilever with UDL or UVL, or the load–SF–BM relation). Part (b) is a beam from a figure: find the reactions, draw the SFD and BMD, find the maximum BM and the point of contraflexure. Neat, labelled diagrams with values carry most of the marks.

1. What is a beam?

A beam is a structural member that carries loads perpendicular (transverse) to its axis. The loads try to bend the beam. A bar in Unit 1 carried its load along the axis; a beam carries it across the axis.

Inside the beam, two internal actions resist the loads at every cross-section: a shear force (which tries to slide one part past the other) and a bending moment (which tries to bend the beam). This unit finds both of them along the length.

2. Types of supports and their reactions

A support holds the beam in place. It gives reaction forces (and sometimes a reaction moment). The type of support decides how many reactions it gives.

SupportWhat it stopsReactions
Simple support / rollerMovement normal to the surface only. The beam can slide and rotate.1: a force normal to the surface (vertical for a horizontal beam)
Hinge (pin)Movement in every direction. The beam can rotate.2: vertical \( R_V \) and horizontal \( R_H \)
Fixed (built-in)Movement and rotation.3: \( R_V \), \( R_H \) and a fixing moment \( M_A \)

A plane beam has three equations of equilibrium: \( \sum F_x = 0 \), \( \sum F_y = 0 \), \( \sum M = 0 \). So the statics can find at most three unknown reactions. With only vertical loads, \( R_H = 0 \) and two equations remain: \( \sum F_y = 0 \) and \( \sum M = 0 \).

3. Types of beams

  • Cantilever: fixed at one end and free at the other. Example: a balcony slab, a crane jib, a diving board.
  • Simply supported beam: rests on a hinge at one end and a roller at the other. Example: a plank across a stream, a bridge girder.
  • Overhanging beam: simply supported, but one or both ends project beyond the supports. The projecting part is the overhang.
  • Fixed (built-in) beam: both ends fixed.
  • Propped cantilever: a cantilever with an extra simple support (prop) at the free end.
  • Continuous beam: rests on more than two supports.
Determinate and indeterminate. A beam is statically determinate when the equations of statics alone give all the reactions: cantilever, simply supported and overhanging beams. Fixed, propped and continuous beams have more unknown reactions than equations, so they are statically indeterminate. This unit uses only determinate beams.

4. Types of loads

  • Point (concentrated) load W (kN): acts at one point. A wheel, a column on a beam.
  • Uniformly distributed load (UDL) w (kN/m): the same intensity along a length. Self-weight, a floor slab.
  • Uniformly varying load (UVL): the intensity changes linearly, for example from zero at one end to w at the other (triangular load). Water pressure on a wall, earth pressure.
  • Applied couple (moment) M (kN·m): a turning effect at one point, for example from a bracket fixed on the beam.

To find the reactions, replace each distributed load by its resultant at the centroid of the load diagram:

\[ \text{UDL: } W = wL \text{ at } \tfrac{L}{2} \qquad \text{UVL (0 to } w\text{): } W = \tfrac{1}{2}wL \text{ at } \tfrac{L}{3} \text{ from the larger end} \]

A trapezoidal load (w₁ to w₂) = a UDL of w₁ + a triangle from 0 to (w₂ − w₁).

Common mistake. Use the resultant only to find the reactions, or for the part of the load on one side of the section. Never put the resultant of the whole UDL into the BM at a section inside the UDL.

5. Shear force and bending moment

Cut the beam at a section X–X at distance x from the left end (Fig. 2.4). Each part must stay in equilibrium, so the other part applies an internal force and an internal moment at the cut.

Shear force (SF) at a section is the algebraic sum of all the vertical forces (loads and reactions) on either side of the section. Bending moment (BM) at a section is the algebraic sum of the moments, about the section, of all the forces on either side of the section.

\[ F_x = R_A - W_1 \qquad M_x = R_A\,x - W_1\,(x - a_1) \]

For Fig. 2.4 with W₁ at distance a₁ from A (left part only). The right part gives the same values. Use the side with fewer forces.

A shear force diagram (SFD) is a graph of SF along the length of the beam. A bending moment diagram (BMD) is a graph of BM along the length. Draw both under the beam, to the same horizontal scale, with the values at all key points.

6. Sign convention

  • Positive SF: the resultant force on the left part acts upward (and on the right part downward). The two parts try to rotate clockwise.
  • Negative SF: the resultant on the left part acts downward.
  • Positive BM (sagging): the beam bends concave upward, like a smile. The moment on the left part is clockwise; on the right part it is anticlockwise.
  • Negative BM (hogging): the beam bends convex upward, like a frown.
Working rule. Go from left to right. SF at a section = (upward forces) − (downward forces) on the left. BM at a section = (moments of the upward forces) − (moments of the downward forces) about the section, all from the left part. Then sagging is +, hogging is −. If you work from the right part: SF = (downward forces) − (upward forces) on the right; the BM rule stays the same (upward forces give +, downward forces give −).

A simply supported beam with downward loads sags, so its BM is positive. A cantilever with downward loads hogs, so its BM is negative and largest at the fixed end.

7. Relation between load, shear force and bending moment

Take a small element of length dx from a beam with a distributed load w per unit length (downward). Fig. 2.6 shows the forces on it, all drawn positive.

  1. Vertical equilibrium: \( F - w\,dx - (F + dF) = 0 \), so \( dF = -w\,dx \).
  2. Moments about C on the right face: \( M + F\,dx - w\,dx\cdot\frac{dx}{2} - (M + dM) = 0 \). Neglect \( (dx)^2 \), so \( dM = F\,dx \).
\[ \frac{dF}{dx} = -w \qquad\qquad \frac{dM}{dx} = F \qquad\qquad \frac{d^2M}{dx^2} = -w \]

x measured from the left end, w downward positive, sign convention of Section 6.

What these relations tell you:

  • The slope of the SFD at a section = − (load intensity) there. No load → SFD horizontal.
  • The slope of the BMD at a section = SF there. So BM is maximum (or minimum) where SF = 0 or where SF changes sign.
  • Change in SF between two sections = − (area of the load diagram between them).
  • Change in BM between two sections = area of the SFD between them. Use this as a quick check.
  • A point load is not covered by the equations: it makes a sudden jump in the SFD equal to the load. An applied couple makes a sudden jump in the BMD equal to the couple.

Memory trick. The BMD is always one degree higher than the SFD, and the SFD is one degree higher than the load: load (0, 1) → SF (1, 2) → BM (2, 3).

9. Maximum bending moment and point of contraflexure

The beam is designed for the maximum bending moment. Find it at the section where the SF is zero or changes sign. In a segment with a UDL, put the SF expression equal to zero and solve for x. At a point load, check whether the SF changes sign from + to −.

The point of contraflexure is the point where the bending moment changes sign (from sagging to hogging or back). The BM is zero there. The bent shape of the beam changes its curvature at this point. It occurs in overhanging beams, fixed and continuous beams, and beams with an applied couple. At an applied couple the BM can jump from + to − (or back) with no zero value; that point is also a point of contraflexure (Fig. 2.14). A simply supported beam with only downward loads has no point of contraflexure.

Real world. In reinforced concrete, the steel bars must be on the tension side. Sagging puts tension at the bottom and hogging at the top, so the designer bends the bars up near the points of contraflexure. A construction joint is often placed near a point of contraflexure because the BM is small there.

10. Method to draw the SFD and BMD

  1. Draw the beam with all loads and dimensions. Name the key points A, B, C… at supports, point loads, couples and the ends of distributed loads.
  2. Find the reactions: \( \sum M = 0 \) about one support, then \( \sum F_y = 0 \). Check with moments about the other support. A cantilever needs no reactions if you start from the free end.
  3. SF: start at the left end and go to the right. Add each upward force, subtract each downward force. Find the SF just to the left and just to the right of every point load.
  4. BM: find the BM at every key point (zero at a free end and at a simple end support).
  5. Find x where SF = 0 inside a distributed load. Find the BM there: this is the maximum BM.
  6. For overhanging beams, put the BM expression equal to zero to find the points of contraflexure.
  7. Draw the SFD and BMD under the beam. Join the points with the correct shape (Section 8). Mark +, −, the values, the maximum BM and the contraflexure points.
Check. The SFD must close to zero at the right end, and the BM must be zero at a free end or a simple end support. If not, there is an error in the reactions.

11. Standard cases: cantilever

For a cantilever, work from the free end. Then the reactions are not needed. Measure x from the free end B and use the part between the section and B.

(a) Point load W at the free end. At distance x from B: F = +W (constant), M = −W x (linear).

\[ F = W \;(\text{constant}), \qquad M_x = -Wx, \qquad M_{\max} = -WL \text{ at the fixed end A} \]

(b) UDL w over the whole span. The load on the part of length x is wx, and it acts at x/2 from the section.

\[ F_x = wx, \qquad M_x = -\frac{wx^2}{2}, \qquad F_A = wL, \qquad M_A = -\frac{wL^2}{2} \]

(c) UVL from zero at the free end to w at the fixed end. At distance x from B the intensity is wx/L. The load on length x is the triangle area \( \frac{1}{2}\cdot x \cdot \frac{wx}{L} = \frac{wx^2}{2L} \). It acts at x/3 from the section.

\[ F_x = \frac{wx^2}{2L}, \qquad M_x = -\frac{wx^3}{6L}, \qquad F_A = \frac{wL}{2}, \qquad M_A = -\frac{wL^2}{6} \]

12. Standard cases: simply supported beam

Find the reactions first. Then measure x from the left support A.

(a) Central point load W. By symmetry \( R_A = R_B = W/2 \). For AC, M = Wx/2, which grows to WL/4 at C.

\[ R_A = R_B = \frac{W}{2}, \qquad M_{\max} = \frac{WL}{4} \text{ at the centre} \]

(b) Eccentric point load W at distance a from A and b from B (a + b = L). Moments about B: \( R_A L = Wb \).

\[ R_A = \frac{Wb}{L}, \qquad R_B = \frac{Wa}{L}, \qquad M_{\max} = M_C = \frac{Wab}{L} \]

(c) UDL w over the whole span. \( R_A = R_B = wL/2 \). At x: \( F_x = \frac{wL}{2} - wx \) and \( M_x = \frac{wL}{2}x - \frac{wx^2}{2} \). F = 0 at x = L/2.

\[ M_{\max} = \frac{wL}{2}\cdot\frac{L}{2} - \frac{w}{2}\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{wL^2}{8} \text{ at the centre} \]

Try the beam below. Set the point load to 0 and see the parabola of a pure UDL; then add a point load and watch where the SF changes sign.

(d) UVL from zero at A to w at B. Total load wL/2 acts at 2L/3 from A. So \( R_B = \frac{wL}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = \frac{wL}{3} \) and \( R_A = \frac{wL}{6} \). At x the load on the left part is \( \frac{wx^2}{2L} \) at x/3 from the section.

\[ F_x = \frac{wL}{6} - \frac{wx^2}{2L}, \qquad M_x = \frac{wL}{6}x - \frac{wx^3}{6L} \]

F = 0 at \( x = L/\sqrt{3} = 0.577L \). Then \( M_{\max} = \frac{wL^2}{9\sqrt{3}} = 0.0642\,wL^2 \).

(e) Clockwise couple M at C, distance a from A. Moments about A: \( R_B L = M \), so \( R_B = M/L \) upward and \( R_A = M/L \) downward. The SF is −M/L everywhere. The BM falls to −Ma/L just left of C, jumps up by M to +Mb/L just right of C, and falls to zero at B.

13. Overhanging beam with UDL

Example: beam ABC with supports at A and B (AB = 4 m) and an overhang BC = 2 m. A UDL of 10 kN/m acts on the whole 6 m.

  • Moments about A: \( R_B \times 4 = 60 \times 3 \), so \( R_B = 45 \) kN and \( R_A = 60 - 45 = 15 \) kN.
  • SF: +15 at A; 15 − 40 = −25 just left of B; −25 + 45 = +20 just right of B; 0 at C.
  • SF = 0 at 15 − 10x = 0, x = 1.5 m. \( M_{\max} = 15(1.5) - 5(1.5)^2 = 11.25 \) kN·m (sagging).
  • At B (from the right): \( M_B = -10 \times 2 \times 1 = -20 \) kN·m (hogging).
  • Contraflexure: \( 15x - 5x^2 = 0 \), so x = 3 m from A.
Overhang rule. An overhang gives a hogging BM over its support. The SF changes sign at that support, so the BM there is a "maximum negative". Always report both: the maximum positive BM (in the span) and the maximum negative BM (at the support).

  2. a parabola
  3. an inclined straight line
  4. a cubic curve

  1. the SF is maximum
  2. the SF is zero or changes sign
  3. the load is maximum
  4. the deflection is zero

  2. wL²/4

  1. −wL²/2
  2. −wL²/3

  4. −wL²/6

  1. horizontal line and inclined line
  2. parabola and cubic
  3. inclined line and parabola
  4. inclined line and cubic

Load of degree 0 → SF of degree 1 → BM of degree 2.

A point of contraflexure is a point where

  1. the SF changes sign
  2. the BM changes sign
  3. the SF is maximum
  4. the load is applied

At a point of contraflexure the BM is zero and changes from sagging to hogging (or back).

  1. a sudden jump in the BMD only
  2. a sudden jump in the SFD only
  3. a jump in both SFD and BMD
  4. no change in either diagram

A couple has no net force, so the SF does not change. The BM jumps by the value of the couple.

Theory questions

Q1. Derive a relationship between load intensity, shear force and bending moment with usual notations.

Jan 2023 (CBCS) Q4(a) · Feb-Mar 2022 (CBCS) Q4(a)

Element dx with all forces drawn positive, two equilibrium equations, the two results, and what they mean for the diagrams.

Model answer

Notation. w = intensity of load (N/m, downward positive), F = shear force, M = bending moment, x = distance from the left end. Positive SF and sagging BM as per the usual sign convention.

Consider a beam carrying a distributed load. Take a small element of length dx between two sections at x and x + dx. Over the small length dx the load intensity is taken as constant, w.

1. Relation between load and shear force. For vertical equilibrium of the element, \( \sum F_y = 0 \):

\[ F - w\,dx - (F + dF) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; dF = -w\,dx \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{dF}{dx} = -w \]

The rate of change of shear force with x is equal to the intensity of load (with a negative sign for a downward load).

2. Relation between shear force and bending moment. Take moments about point C on the right face, \( \sum M_C = 0 \):

\[ M + F\,dx - w\,dx\cdot\frac{dx}{2} - (M + dM) = 0 \]

The term \( w(dx)^2/2 \) is a small quantity of the second order; neglect it:

\[ dM = F\,dx \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{dM}{dx} = F \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{d^2M}{dx^2} = \frac{dF}{dx} = -w \]

The rate of change of bending moment is equal to the shear force at the section.

Conclusions (applications).

  • BM is maximum or minimum where \( dM/dx = 0 \), that is, where the SF is zero or changes sign.
  • Between two sections 1 and 2: \( F_2 - F_1 = -\int w\,dx \) (area of the load diagram) and \( M_2 - M_1 = \int F\,dx \) (area of the SFD).
  • Where w = 0, F is constant and M is linear. Where w is constant (UDL), F is linear and M is parabolic. Where w is linear (UVL), F is parabolic and M is cubic.
  • A point load gives a jump in the SFD; an applied couple gives a jump in the BMD.

Q2. Draw the SFD and BMD of a simply supported beam carrying a uniformly distributed load of w per unit length over the whole span L.

Aug-Sep 2023 Q5(a) · Nov 2024 Q5(a)

Reactions, expressions for F and M at x, values at A, centre and B, the maximum BM wL²/8, and the neat diagrams.

Model answer

Consider a simply supported beam AB of span L carrying a UDL of w per unit length over the whole span.

Reactions. Total load = wL at the centre. By symmetry, \( R_A = R_B = \dfrac{wL}{2} \).

Shear force. Section X–X at distance x from A (left part):

\[ F_x = R_A - wx = \frac{wL}{2} - wx \]

This is linear in x. At x = 0: \( F_A = +wL/2 \). At x = L/2: F = 0. At x = L: \( F_B = -wL/2 \). The SFD is a straight line from +wL/2 to −wL/2.

Bending moment.

\[ M_x = R_A\,x - wx\cdot\frac{x}{2} = \frac{wL}{2}x - \frac{wx^2}{2} \]

This is a parabola. At x = 0 and x = L: M = 0. The BM is maximum where F = 0, at x = L/2:

\[ M_{\max} = \frac{wL}{2}\cdot\frac{L}{2} - \frac{w}{2}\cdot\frac{L^2}{4} = \frac{wL^2}{8} \]

Result. Max SF = wL/2 at the supports; max BM = wL²/8 (sagging) at the centre; no point of contraflexure.

Q3. Draw the SFD and BMD of a simply supported beam of span L carrying a uniformly varying load from zero at one end to w per unit length at the other end.

Nov-Dec 2025 Q5(a) · Jun-Jul 2024 Q5(a)

Reactions wL/6 and wL/3, F and M expressions, the position x = L/√3 of zero SF and the maximum BM wL²/(9√3).

Model answer

Consider a simply supported beam AB of span L. The load is zero at A and increases uniformly to w per unit length at B.

Reactions. Total load = area of the load triangle = \( \frac{1}{2}wL \). It acts at the centroid, 2L/3 from A. Moments about A:

\[ R_B \cdot L = \frac{wL}{2}\cdot\frac{2L}{3} \Rightarrow R_B = \frac{wL}{3}, \qquad R_A = \frac{wL}{2} - \frac{wL}{3} = \frac{wL}{6} \]

Shear force. At section X–X at distance x from A, the load intensity is \( \frac{wx}{L} \). The load on the left part = \( \frac{1}{2}\cdot x\cdot\frac{wx}{L} = \frac{wx^2}{2L} \), acting at x/3 from the section.

\[ F_x = \frac{wL}{6} - \frac{wx^2}{2L} \]

This is a parabola (2nd degree). At x = 0: F = +wL/6. At x = L: F = wL/6 − wL/2 = −wL/3.

Position of zero SF. \( \frac{wL}{6} = \frac{wx^2}{2L} \Rightarrow x^2 = \frac{L^2}{3} \Rightarrow x = \frac{L}{\sqrt{3}} = 0.577L \) from A.

Bending moment.

\[ M_x = \frac{wL}{6}x - \frac{wx^2}{2L}\cdot\frac{x}{3} = \frac{wL}{6}x - \frac{wx^3}{6L} \]

This is a cubic curve, zero at A and B. The maximum is at x = L/√3:

\[ M_{\max} = \frac{wL}{6}\cdot\frac{L}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{w}{6L}\cdot\frac{L^3}{3\sqrt{3}} = \frac{wL^2}{6\sqrt{3}} - \frac{wL^2}{18\sqrt{3}} = \frac{wL^2}{9\sqrt{3}} = 0.0642\,wL^2 \]
Note. The maximum BM is not at mid-span. It is nearer the heavier end B. At mid-span, M = wL²/16 = 0.0625 wL², which is a little less than 0.0642 wL².

Q4. Draw the SFD and BMD for a cantilever of span L carrying (i) a uniformly distributed load of w per unit length over the entire span, (ii) a uniformly varying load from zero at the free end to w per unit length at the fixed end.

Jan 2025 Q5(a) · Nov 2024 Q6(a)

Work from the free end; give F and M at distance x, the values at the fixed end, and the curve shapes.

Model answer

Cantilever AB: fixed at A, free at B. Take a section X–X at distance x from the free end B and consider the part between X–X and B. With x from the free end, a downward load on the right part gives positive SF and hogging (negative) BM.

(i) UDL w over the whole span. Load on the part of length x = wx, acting at x/2 from the section.

\[ F_x = +wx, \qquad M_x = -wx\cdot\frac{x}{2} = -\frac{wx^2}{2} \]
  • At B (x = 0): F = 0, M = 0.
  • At A (x = L): \( F_A = wL \), \( M_A = -\dfrac{wL^2}{2} \).
  • SFD: inclined straight line. BMD: parabola, concave as shown, maximum (hogging) at the fixed end.

(ii) UVL, zero at B, w at A. At distance x from B the intensity is wx/L. Load on length x = \( \frac{1}{2}\cdot x\cdot\frac{wx}{L} = \frac{wx^2}{2L} \), acting at x/3 from the section.

\[ F_x = \frac{wx^2}{2L}, \qquad M_x = -\frac{wx^2}{2L}\cdot\frac{x}{3} = -\frac{wx^3}{6L} \]
  • At B (x = 0): F = 0, M = 0.
  • At A (x = L): \( F_A = \dfrac{wL}{2} \), \( M_A = -\dfrac{wL^2}{6} \).
  • SFD: parabola (2nd degree). BMD: cubic curve (3rd degree).

Fixed-end reactions. (i) \( R_A = wL \), \( M_A = wL^2/2 \); (ii) \( R_A = wL/2 \), \( M_A = wL^2/6 \). There is no point of contraflexure in either case.

Q5. What are the different types of beams and loads? Define shear force, bending moment, SFD and point of contraflexure, and state the sign conventions.

Mar-Apr 2021 (CBCS) Q1(8), Q1(9) · Jan 2023 (CBCS) Q1 · Syllabus topic

Sketches of the six beams, the four loads, exact definitions and the sign sketches. Often asked as 2-mark parts.

Model answer

Beam. A beam is a structural member subjected to loads acting transverse (perpendicular) to its longitudinal axis, which it resists by bending.

Types of beams (depending on the supports):

  1. Cantilever: one end fixed, the other end free.
  2. Simply supported: both ends rest on simple supports (hinge and roller).
  3. Overhanging: one or both ends extend beyond the supports.
  4. Fixed: both ends rigidly fixed.
  5. Propped cantilever: fixed at one end, simply supported at the other.
  6. Continuous: supported at more than two points.

Types 1–3 are statically determinate; types 4–6 are statically indeterminate.

Types of loads: (i) point or concentrated load, (ii) uniformly distributed load (UDL), (iii) uniformly varying load (UVL: triangular or trapezoidal), (iv) applied couple or moment.

Shear force at a section: the algebraic sum of all the vertical forces acting on either side of the section.

Bending moment at a section: the algebraic sum of the moments of all the forces acting on either side of the section, taken about that section.

Shear force diagram (SFD): a diagram that shows the variation of shear force along the length of the beam. Bending moment diagram (BMD): a diagram that shows the variation of bending moment along the length of the beam.

Point of contraflexure: the point in a beam where the bending moment changes its sign (from sagging to hogging or vice versa); the BM there is zero. It is also called the point of inflexion.

Sign conventions.

  • SF is positive when the resultant of the forces to the left of the section is upward (or to the right is downward); otherwise negative.
  • BM is positive (sagging) when it makes the beam concave upward: clockwise moment of the left-hand forces or anticlockwise moment of the right-hand forces. Negative BM is hogging (convex upward).

Numericals

Method. (1) Draw the beam and name the key points. (2) Reactions: \( \sum M = 0 \) about one support, \( \sum F_y = 0 \), check with the other support (cantilever: start at the free end, or \( R_A = \sum W \), \( M_A = \sum W\cdot d \)). (3) SF just left and just right of every key point. (4) BM at every key point. (5) SF = 0 inside a distributed load → x → maximum BM. (6) BM = 0 → points of contraflexure. (7) Draw the SFD and BMD under the beam with all values. Units: kN, m, kN·m. x is measured from the left end unless stated.
