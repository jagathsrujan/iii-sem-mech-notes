Machine Tools

Machine tools hold the workpiece and the tool and give the motions for metal cutting. This unit covers the basic elements, the classification, and the lathe, milling, drilling, shaping and grinding machines with their operations.

Concepts

Exam pattern. Unit 4 asks for labelled sketches of machines (lathe, milling, radial drilling, cylindrical grinder) and short notes on operations. Each answer needs a neat sketch and parts in points. Learn the sketches first.

1. Introduction to basic metal cutting

Metal cutting removes extra metal from a workpiece, as chips, with a hard cutting tool. The result is the required shape, size and surface finish. A machine tool is a power-driven machine that holds the tool and the workpiece and gives the relative motions for cutting.

Fig. 4.1 shows the three basic motions:

  • Cutting (primary) motion: gives the cutting speed V. The workpiece turns on a lathe; the cutter turns on a milling machine; the tool moves on a shaper.
  • Feed motion: moves the cutting point to new metal. Unit: mm/rev or mm/stroke.
  • Depth of cut d: thickness of metal removed in one pass, in mm.

D = workpiece diameter (mm), N = speed (rpm), L = length of cut (mm), f = feed (mm/rev), T = machining time

2. Basic elements of machine tools

Every machine tool has the same basic elements. Fig. 4.2 shows how they work together.

  • Bed, frame or column: a rigid cast-iron structure. It carries all parts and has accurate guideways. Cast iron damps vibration.
  • Work-holding device: holds the workpiece. Examples: chuck, centres, face plate (lathe); vice, T-slot table (milling, drilling); magnetic chuck (grinding).
  • Tool-holding device: holds the tool. Examples: tool post (lathe), arbor (milling), drill chuck or Morse taper (drilling), clapper box (shaper).
  • Drive: motor, belts and gearbox. It gives a range of spindle speeds.
  • Feed mechanism: lead screw, feed rod, rack and pinion. It gives the feed in mm/rev.
  • Controls: levers, handwheels and dials to set speed, feed and depth.

We classify machine tools by the number of cutting edges, by the use (general, single or special purpose) and by automation (manual, semi-automatic, automatic, CNC). Fig. 4.3 groups them by the cutting tool.

4. Centre lathe

The centre lathe holds the workpiece between centres or in a chuck and turns it. A single-point tool, on the carriage, moves along or across the work. The size is given by the swing over the bed and the distance between centres.

5. Milling machines

Milling removes metal with a rotating multi-tooth cutter while the workpiece is fed. In up milling the cutter turns against the feed. In down milling it turns with the feed (Fig. 4.6).

PointUp millingDown milling
Chip thickness0 at start, maximum at endMaximum at start, 0 at end
Cutting force on workUpward; tends to lift itDownward; holds it on the table
BacklashNo problemNeeds a backlash eliminator
Surface finishPoorerBetter
Tool lifeShorter (rubbing at start)Longer
UseCastings with a hard skin; old machinesRigid machines, finishing, thin work

Face milling uses a large cutter with its axis at right angles to the face. End milling uses a cutter with teeth on the end and sides for slots, pockets and profiles (Fig. 4.7).

The column-and-knee machine is the most common milling machine. The knee moves up and down the column, the saddle moves across, and the table moves along (Fig. 4.8).

6. Drilling machines

A drilling machine makes round holes with a twist drill. It turns the drill and feeds it along its axis. The radial drilling machine has an arm that swings round a column. The drill head slides on the arm. We drill large, heavy parts without moving them (Fig. 4.9).

Other hole-making operations use other tools on the same machine (Fig. 4.10).

7. Shaping machine

The shaper cuts flat surfaces, slots and keyways with a single-point tool on a reciprocating ram. The tool cuts on the forward stroke only. The table feeds the work a small step at the end of each return stroke. The return stroke is faster because of the quick-return mechanism.

Fig. 4.12 shows the crank and slotted link mechanism. The bull gear turns at constant speed. The crank pin moves the rocker arm. The cutting stroke takes 240° of the crank turn; the return takes 120°.

r = crank radius, d = distance from the gear centre to the rocker pivot. For θ = 30° the ratio is 2 : 1

8. Grinding machines

Grinding removes very small chips with the hard grains of a rotating wheel. It gives fine finish and close tolerance, and it cuts hardened steel. A grinding wheel is a mass of abrasive grains held together by a bond, with pores between them (Fig. 4.13).

ParameterMeaningCommon choices
AbrasiveMaterial of the grainA = aluminium oxide (steels); C = silicon carbide (cast iron, non-ferrous); also CBN, diamond
Grain sizeNumber of sieve holes per inchCoarse 8–24; medium 30–60; fine 70–180; very fine 220–600
GradeHardness: how firmly the bond holds the grainA–H soft; I–P medium; Q–Z hard
StructureSpacing of the grains1 dense to 15 open
BondMaterial that holds the grainsV vitrified; S silicate; B resinoid; R rubber; E shellac
Memory trick. Read the mark in order: Abrasive – Grain – Grade – Structure – Bond. Example: A 60 K 5 V.

The plain cylindrical grinder grinds the outside of a work held between centres. The wheel turns fast. The work turns slowly. The table traverses the work past the wheel, and the wheel is fed in after each pass (Fig. 4.14).

Common mistake. A hard-grade wheel suits soft material, and a soft-grade wheel suits hard material. Hard material blunts the grains fast, so the wheel must release them.

Key terms

Machine tool
Power-driven machine that holds work and tool and gives the motions for cutting.
Feed
Movement of the tool per revolution or stroke to reach new metal, in mm/rev.
Swing
Largest diameter of work that a lathe can turn over its bed.
Lead screw
Accurate screw on a lathe that moves the carriage for threading.
Up milling
Cutter turns against the feed; chip grows from 0 to maximum.
Down milling
Cutter turns with the feed; chip falls from maximum to 0.
Counterbore / countersink
Flat-bottom step / conical seat at the mouth of a hole.
Quick-return ratio
Cutting-stroke time divided by return-stroke time; more than 1 on a shaper.
Grade
Hardness of a grinding wheel: the grip of the bond on the grains.
Dressing and truing
Dressing cleans a clogged wheel; truing restores its shape.

Theory questions

Q1. Sketch a Centre Lathe and mention its various parts.

MT QB Q33

Labelled sketch (Fig. 4.4), then each part with its function.

Model answer

Definition. The centre lathe is a general-purpose machine tool that holds the work between centres or in a chuck, turns it, and cuts it with a single-point tool that moves along or across the axis.

Fig. 4.4 shows the labelled sketch.

  • Bed: cast-iron base with guideways; carries the headstock, carriage and tailstock.
  • Headstock: holds the hollow spindle, the chuck and the speed gearbox.
  • Tailstock: slides on the bed; its quill holds a dead centre or a drill; it can be set over for taper turning.
  • Carriage: carries the tool. It has the saddle (slides on the bed), the cross slide (moves across), the compound rest (swivels for tapers) with the tool post, and the apron (feed gears and half-nut).
  • Lead screw: moves the carriage for thread cutting.
  • Feed rod: gives the power feed for turning and facing.
  • Legs: support the bed on the floor.

Size: swing over the bed and the distance between centres. Use: turning, facing, threading, boring and drilling of shafts, pins and bushes.

Q2. What are the various operations that can be performed on a lathe? Explain any four in detail.

MT QB Q34

Name the nine operations; explain four with a sketch (Fig. 4.5): tool, motion, use.

Model answer

Operations. Turning, facing, taper turning, threading, knurling, drilling, boring, parting off and chamfering. Fig. 4.5 shows all of them. Four in detail:

  1. Turning. The work rotates and the tool feeds along the axis. It reduces the diameter and makes a cylinder. Depth of cut d = (D − d₁)/2.
  2. Facing. The tool feeds at right angles to the axis, from the edge to the centre. It makes a flat face square to the axis, and sets the length of the part.
  3. Taper turning. It makes a cone. Methods: compound rest set to α/2 (short steep tapers), tailstock set-over S = L(D − d)/2l (long gentle tapers), taper attachment, and form tool.
  4. Threading. A 60° tool (V thread) moves one pitch per revolution of the work. The lead screw and the half-nut give this exact feed. Many passes are made, each deeper.

Also: Knurling presses a diamond pattern for grip; drilling uses a drill in the tailstock; boring enlarges a hole with a boring bar; parting cuts off the work with a thin blade; chamfering bevels a corner (usually 45°).

Q4. Write a short note on (a) face milling (b) end milling.

MT QB Q36

Two short notes with the sketch (Fig. 4.7). About 5 marks each.

Model answer

Fig. 4.7 shows both operations.

(a) Face milling. A face mill, with teeth or inserts on its periphery and end, has its axis at right angles to the surface. The end teeth finish the surface; the peripheral teeth remove the bulk. Used on vertical or horizontal machines.

  • Cutter diameter is larger than the width of the work.
  • Gives a flat, smooth face at a high removal rate.
  • Used for large flat surfaces, such as the tops of castings.

(b) End milling. An end mill is a shank cutter with flutes. It has cutting edges on the end and the sides, and it is held in a collet on a vertical spindle.

  • Cuts slots, keyways, pockets, steps and profiles.
  • Can plunge into the work like a drill.
  • Used on tool and die work.

Q5. Sketch a Column and Knee type milling machine and explain.

MT QB Q37

Labelled sketch (Fig. 4.8), parts and the three feeds.

Model answer

Definition. The column-and-knee machine has a vertical column with a knee that slides on its front guideways. It is the most common milling machine. Fig. 4.8 shows a plain horizontal type.

  • Base: heavy casting; holds the column and the coolant.
  • Column: vertical frame; houses the spindle and the drive.
  • Overarm: at the top; supports the arbor support.
  • Arbor: shaft that holds the cutter; the arbor support holds its outer end.
  • Knee: moves up and down the column; carries the saddle.
  • Saddle: on the knee; gives the cross feed.
  • Table: on the saddle; has T-slots; gives the longitudinal feed.
  • Elevating screw: raises the knee.

Types: plain (horizontal), vertical and universal. Use: flat faces, slots, gears and keyways.

Q6. With a neat sketch explain the radial drilling machine (QB: “radial milling machine”).

MT QB Q38

Labelled sketch (Fig. 4.9), parts, motions and use.

Model answer
Data note. The question bank says “radial milling machine”. No such standard machine exists in this syllabus. The notes treat it as the radial drilling machine.

Definition. The radial drilling machine has a drill head that moves on a radial arm. The arm swings round a column. It drills large heavy work that cannot be moved. Fig. 4.9 shows the sketch.

  • Base: heavy casting; carries the column and the worktable.
  • Column: vertical cylinder; the arm sleeve turns and slides on it.
  • Radial arm: horizontal arm with guideways; raised or lowered by the elevating screw.
  • Drill head: slides along the arm; holds the motor, the gearbox and the spindle.
  • Spindle: carries the drill (Morse taper) and feeds down.
  • Worktable: holds the work.

Motions: the arm swings round the column, moves up or down, and the head slides along the arm. So the drill reaches any point in a large area. Use: large castings and fabrications.

Q7. Discuss any four drilling operations with a neat sketch.

MT QB Q39

Four operations from Fig. 4.10: tool, result, use.

Model answer

Fig. 4.10 shows seven operations. Four in detail:

  1. Drilling. A twist drill makes a new round hole. The drill rotates and feeds down.
  2. Reaming. A multi-flute reamer removes 0.1 to 0.5 mm from a drilled hole. It gives an accurate size and a good finish.
  3. Counterboring. A tool with a pilot enlarges the top of a hole to a flat-bottom step. The step seats a bolt head or nut.
  4. Countersinking. A cone tool (usually 90°) cuts a conical seat for a flat-head screw.

Also: boring enlarges a hole with a single-point tool; tapping cuts internal threads with a tap; spot facing makes a flat seat on a rough surface.

Q8. What is a grinding wheel? List the grinding wheel parameters that influence the grinding performance.

MT QB Q40

Define; sketch (Fig. 4.13); five parameters with the marking system.

Model answer

Definition. A grinding wheel is a cutting tool made of hard abrasive grains held together by a bond, with pores between them. Each grain acts as a small cutting edge. Fig. 4.13 shows the structure.

Parameters:

  1. Abrasive: aluminium oxide (A) for steels; silicon carbide (C) for cast iron and non-ferrous; CBN and diamond for very hard material.
  2. Grain size: coarse (8–24) removes fast; fine (70–600) gives a good finish.
  3. Grade: bond strength, A (soft) to Z (hard). Soft wheels for hard work; hard wheels for soft work.
  4. Structure: grain spacing, 1 (dense) to 15 (open). Open structure gives chip room.
  5. Bond: V vitrified (most common), S silicate, B resinoid, R rubber, E shellac.

Marking (IS 551): A 60 K 5 V means aluminium oxide, grain 60 (medium), grade K (medium), structure 5 (medium), vitrified bond. A wrong choice causes glazing (blunt grains) or loading (clogged pores). Dressing cleans the wheel and truing restores its shape.

Q9. Explain the plain cylindrical grinding machine with a neat sketch.

MT QB Q41

Labelled sketch (Fig. 4.14), parts and the four motions.

Model answer

Definition. A plain cylindrical grinder grinds the outer cylindrical surface of a workpiece held between centres. Fig. 4.14 shows the sketch.

  • Bed: rigid base carrying the table and the wheel slide.
  • Table: slides on the bed; swivels a few degrees for slight tapers.
  • Headstock (work head): drives the work at low speed.
  • Footstock: supports the other end with a centre.
  • Wheel head and slide: carry the motor and the grinding wheel; fed in by a handwheel.

Working. The wheel turns at high speed. The work turns slowly in the opposite sense at the contact. The table traverses the work past the wheel. After each pass the wheel is fed in (infeed) a few hundredths of a millimetre. Coolant is applied.

Use: shafts, spindles and pins that need a fine finish and close tolerance.

Q10. Explain the shaping machine: principle, parts and the quick-return mechanism.

Syllabus topic

Sketch the machine (Fig. 4.11) and the mechanism (Fig. 4.12); give the ratio.

Model answer

Principle. A single-point tool on a reciprocating ram cuts a flat surface. Cutting happens on the forward stroke only. The table feeds the work a small step at the end of each return stroke. Fig. 4.11 shows the machine.

  • Base and column: support all parts; the column holds the drive gears.
  • Ram: carries the tool head and moves to and fro.
  • Tool head: has the clapper box and tool post; it gives the down feed.
  • Cross rail, saddle and table: hold the vice and give the cross feed.

Quick-return mechanism (crank and slotted link, Fig. 4.12). The bull gear turns at constant speed. The crank pin slides in the rocker arm and swings it about the pivot. The pin turns through 240° during cutting and 120° during return, so the ratio is 2 : 1. In general ratio = (180° + 2θ)/(180° − 2θ), with sin θ = r/d.

Use: flat faces, slots, keyways and dovetails. The size is the maximum stroke length.
